# Data patterns: what the synthetic data supports

Analysed on 2026-10-19 by four agents over one DuckDB base built from the ČNB files. This page is the input for the rule specs in [`../rules/`](../rules/README.md) and for the 2-page decision-logic write-up. Research basis: [aml-pattern-research.md](aml-pattern-research.md). The result files and scripts stay in the session scratchpad, outside the repo; the section names below (profile, passthrough, network, artefacts) refer to them.

Client and transaction ids below come from the synthetic set.

## What we ran

- **Base.** All 397,853 July 2026 transactions and 5,493 clients, with every amount converted to CZK at the ČNB fixing on or before the transaction date. The Q2 figures in `clients.csv` are taken as CZK, as their column names say. Expected volume is monthly.
- **Both units.** The generator drew amounts without regard to currency (median about 3,000 in every currency), so every threshold and every planted-pattern check ran twice: on the CZK value and on the raw `amount`. The brief's rules stay in CZK; a pattern planted by the generator would show in the raw amount.
- **Peer groups.** `segment` is client type, AML risk score and contract channel. Where a segment had fewer than 50 clients, the peer group is type and risk with channels merged. That gives 15 groups; the smallest has 24 clients.
- **Hubs.** A counterparty account is a hub when 200 or more clients use it. The cut-off sits in a gap: the 360 hubs are each shared by 260 to 1,372 clients, and every other account by at most 125. Hubs carry 322,308 of the 397,853 transactions.
- **Null models.** Each pattern was compared with what chance gives: rule C with time reversed, fan-in and fan-out with direction labels permuted, shared counterparties with account indices redrawn, amount bands against the fitted amount model.

## Headline: the generator ignores the profile

No planted laundering pattern shows up. The data is one parametric generator with no injected sub-population (artefacts, headline).

Rule A's single-transaction branch is a fixed cut. Because `q2_max` is 8 times the declared ticket, a payment above 2x the Q2 maximum is a raw-amount `z` above `ln 16 = 2.77` standard deviations: 1 in 360 per payment, which happens to about 18 % of clients in a month of about 72 payments. On raw amounts the branch fires on 1,202 tx / 1,009 clients; 1,107 tx / 1,003 clients are exactly the z cut (expected 1,106 / 1,002). The other 95 tx come from the 6 clients contracted in the last 3 days of Q2, whose `q2_max` is 0 to 3 times the ticket: K005492 60, K005489 15, K005488 9, K005491 4, K005493 4, K005490 3 (verified on the base; the tails results list the first five). K005492 has no Q2 activity and should not enter the branch at all (see [rule A](../rules/A-profile-discrepancy.md)). In CZK the branch fires on 74,346 tx and all 5,493 clients (FX); on CZK-currency payments only, 800 tx / 703 clients.

Two statements of the amount model agree. Profile says each July amount is the client's Q2 average transaction × exp(N(0,1)); artefacts says `exp_vol/exp_cnt × exp(N(0,1))`. Because `q2_vol` and `q2_cnt` share one factor f, `q2_vol / q2_cnt = exp_vol / exp_cnt`, so the two are the same quantity. The July average transaction is 1.62x the Q2 average for everyone (profile, observed median); that is the lognormal mean over median, e^0.5 ≈ 1.65 (artefacts, theory).

Other fixed properties of the data:

- Hour of day comes in three quota blocks: 00 to 07 9 %, 08 to 16 82 %, 17 to 23 9 %, uniform inside each block (artefacts G1).
- Dates are uniform over July; day totals are 12,639 to 13,105 (artefacts G1).
- Remittances are outgoing transfers: empty payer account, payer country = client's country in 39,715 of 39,715 rows, payee account = counterparty (passthrough, data facts).
- For CZ clients, a foreign counterparty is the same as a non-CZK currency (101,316 rows foreign and non-CZK, 186,402 CZ and CZK, none mixed) (passthrough, data facts).
- Data quality is clean: no transactions before the contract date, no duplicates, no null rates. The one client with no Q2 activity, K005492, opened on 2026-06-30 (artefacts, data quality).

## What this means for triage and priority

- **Rule A is a ranking problem.** It fires for all 5,493 clients after CZK conversion, and the ratio mostly sorts clients by small `exp_cnt`. Rank on the peer-normalised count gap (`rel_cnt`) and the raw largest payment's peer percentile, not on the CZK ratio. Quote the CZK ratio in `reason`, because that is the brief's form, then the count and raw-amount gaps as the explanation of how much of it is FX.
- **The FX explanation is the documented basis for FP.** Median gap is 13.3x in CZK against 1.85x raw. "Deviation explained by currency conversion, behaviour within the peer group's normal range" is the closure reason.
- **Proposed triage for rule A** (profile section 5). Facts: G95 count or amount gap ≥ peer P95 (340), G90 the same at P90 (651), M raw largest payment ≥ peer P95 (282), C2 CZK-only payment above 2x Q2 max (702), N tenure < 90 days (230), T thin baseline (12), H high risk (392), P PEP (23).
  - Proposal A (recommended): REVIEW if any of G95, M, N, T, H, P. REVIEW 1,150 (behavioural fact only 744, high risk or PEP only 346, both 60); FP-eligible 4,343 (79 %).
  - Proposal B (more recall): G90, M, C2, N, T, H, P. REVIEW 1,856 (behaviour only 1,450, profile only 293, both 113); FP-eligible 3,637.
- **Profile fields carry no behavioural signal.** They still belong in the score, because the brief names them as factors. A Jev question "is this consistent with the profile" can learn nothing beyond the code-computed gap; Jev's useful role is a typed answer on the named risk factors; the readable story stays with the reason template.
- **Rule C has no discriminating power here.** It fires as often with time reversed. Every hit goes to REVIEW as the spec says; priority comes from the scorecard plus the shape points (rows 11, 12), and Jev reads the facts with "consistent / not consistent / cannot tell" and names the missing fact (passthrough, typology section).
- **Proposed priority points** (profile section 6, not fitted; no labels exist): risk high 20, medium 10; PEP 15; count gap ≥ P95 20, P90 to 95 12, P75 to 90 6; raw max payment ≥ P95 10; CZK-only payment above 2x Q2 max 5; tenure < 90 days 10, 90 to 365 days 5; distant 5; B or C on the same client 10 each, at most 20. On the factors computed (no country, no multiple flags): p50 10, p90 25, p99 40, max 65; bands < 20: 4,382, 20 to 39: 1,055, ≥ 40: 56. Details in [priority.md](../rules/priority.md).
- **Missing baseline.** K005492 has no Q2 data; the score script set its percentiles to 1 (maximum discrepancy). Decide that explicitly in code.
- **Demo cases.** K005493 (tenure 1 day, rule C ratio 0.85, inflows TX000098491 and TX000108694, outflows TX000111827 and TX000110786) looks like a textbook flow-through. K003732 has a CZK payment of 4.1 M, 18.4x its Q2 maximum (TX000104688), still inside the lognormal tail.

## Open team decisions

1. **High risk or PEP alone forces REVIEW on rule A.** The data says they behave like everyone else. The brief lists AML risk score and PEP as triage bases, and AMLA draft para 78 rules out automated closure for higher-risk customers. Profile recommends keeping them as REVIEW. In proposal A this is 346 of the 1,150 REVIEW clients.
2. **B or C on the same client forces REVIEW on A.** Not computed with B and C together. Profile recommends yes: "multiple flags" is a named factor.
3. **Remittances as rule C outflow.** A remittance leaves the client to a named payee account, so it meets "outgoing payment to another account". With remittances 1,077 clients, without 720. Passthrough recommends counting them, so a client cannot route the outflow through a remittance service. Record the choice in the decision log.

Nothing beats chance; four rules are kept because their tail probability is stated and their question is one an analyst would ask. New generator facts:

- The declared ticket `exp_vol/exp_cnt` is one lognormal for all client types (mean of ln 8.0, sd 0.8), independent of the declared count. Types differ in declared volume only through the count.
- Risk score follows residence: no client resident outside the EU and EEA is rated low (205 of 205), PEPs are never low (9 high, 14 medium); otherwise about 70 % low, 25 % medium, 5 % high. Only CZ and SK clients are onboarded in person. Risk score, country and channel are one variable for scoring ([priority.md](../rules/priority.md)).
- For full-quarter clients `q2_vol/exp_vol` is uniform on 2.75 to 3.99.
- Contract dates are uniform over calendar days; `client_id` follows contract date.
- Counterparty country is drawn per transaction from one weight vector (CZ 71.95 %, SK 12.01 %, DE 3.64 %, ...) with no home bias. Remittances exclude the client's own country. Currency follows country, with USD at about 35 % for countries outside the EU and EEA.

## Reproduce

The scripts stay in the session scratchpad and are not in the repo; the organisers' data never goes into git.

1. Run `pnpm data:pull` for the raw files.
2. Build the base in DuckDB 1.3.2. FX comes from the ČNB yearly file: pipe-separated, decimal comma, the header gives the quantity per currency (divide the rate by it), CZK has rate 1. Clients get `tenure_days` to 1 July 2026 and `segment` = type, risk and channel. Transactions get the rate by an ASOF join on the last fixing on or before the date, `czk = amount × rate`, `dir` (`in`, `out`, `remit`), and the counterparty account and country (payer for incoming, payee otherwise). A counterparty table counts distinct clients, transactions and CZK per account.
3. Add `peer_group` (segment, or type and risk where a segment has fewer than 50 clients) and `is_hub = n_clients >= 200`. These two steps are not in the base script.
4. Open the base read-only and run each signal on both `czk` and raw `amount`, with its null model.
