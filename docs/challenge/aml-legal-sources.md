# AML legal sources for triage and escalation (2026-10-19)

The rules and guidance behind the FINREG challenge, with links and the provisions our design uses. Researched on 2026-10-19 from primary text unless marked otherwise. Not legal advice. Czech statute text is the consolidated version on zakonyprolidi.cz (latest amendment seen 32/2025 Sb.), not checked against e-sbirka.cz. Translations from Czech are ours.

Timing: the July 2026 data falls under the Czech Act (which transposes AMLD) and the TFR. The AMLR applies from 10 July 2027, and AMLA's monitoring guidelines are a draft. Both show where supervision is heading, so we cite them as direction, not as binding law.

## What each part of the challenge rests on

### Rules A, B and C

- **Rule A, profile discrepancy.** Monitor transactions for consistency with the customer's profile: AMLD Art. 13(1)(d) [EU2], AMLR Art. 26(1) [EU1]. Examine unusually large transactions and unusual patterns: AMLD Art. 18(2) [EU2], AMLR Art. 34(2) [EU1], FATF INR.10 para 20 [G1]. Volume "unexpected based on the information the customer provided at account opening" raises risk: EBA 9.6(b)(iii) [G2].
- **Rule B, high-risk country.** Czech law defines a high-risk third country as one on the EU list, on FATF's "call for action" list, or high risk "for another reason": Act §9(1)(a)(3) [CZ1], Decree §9(3)(a) and §9(4)(a) [CZ2]. FAÚ publishes a combined list, calls it binding and a minimum that banks may extend [CZ8][CZ9]. AMLD Art. 18a speaks of transactions "involving" such countries [EU2], AMLR Art. 29(4) of persons "from" them [EU1]; neither says whether that means payer, payee or residence.
- **Rule B, PEP.** Definition: Act §4(5) [CZ1], AMLD Art. 3(9) to (11) [EU2], AMLR Art. 2(1)(34) [EU1]. Measures are senior management approval, source of wealth and funds, and enhanced ongoing monitoring: AMLD Art. 20(b) [EU2], AMLR Art. 42(1) [EU1], FATF R.12 [G1]. In Czech law, a board member or their AML delegate consents to the relationship: Act §9a(3)(d) [CZ1]; MP 7 says this must not be pushed down to regular staff [CZ5]. PEP measures last at least 12 months after leaving office: Act §54(8) [CZ1].
- **Rule C, flow-through.** No source read names a pass-through or "24 h in, 6 h out" pattern. The closest hooks are transactions with "no economic rationale" (EBA 9.6(b)(iv) [G2]) and unusual patterns (FATF INR.10 para 20 [G1]). UNVERIFIED as a named typology.
- **Further red flag, missing payer information.** The payee's provider must detect missing payer information (TFR Art. 7) and treat it as a factor in judging suspicion (TFR Art. 9) [EU3]. It is not on its own a reason to report.

### Currency conversion

- An EUR amount in the Act means the equivalent at "the ČNB rate valid for the day on which the obligation is performed; if not yet available, the previous day's rate": Act §54(3) [CZ1]. Linked partial payments are added together: §54(4). This governs the Act's own thresholds; we apply it by analogy to the challenge's CZK thresholds.

### Triage and justification

- Every closed alert needs a recorded assessment, whether or not it leads to a report: AMLR Art. 77(1)(b) [EU1]; EBA/GL/2022/05 para 52(d) [G4].
- The closure states whether and why suspicion was excluded, plus any further measures; a reason that can't be reconstructed is a deficiency; a bare "OK" is the typical one: ČNB benchmark p. 15 and footnote 47 [G5].
- Delays and the steps taken to overcome them belong in the record: ČNB p. 13 [G5].
- Draft AMLA guidelines: alerts "assessed without undue delay, prioritised based on risk, and escalated", with a documented rationale (para 75); decision logic understood and documented, with human oversight and validation by sampling (para 79) [EU4].

Mitigating factors that support a false-positive closure: a long-standing client with no earlier concern and activity in line with the profile (EBA 9.7(a) [G2]); counterparties in countries with an equivalent AML regime (EBA 9.9(a) [G2]).

### Automation

- ČNB is technology neutral. It accepts uses from prioritisation up to "fully automated investigation of alerts that are subsequently verified", if the reasons are traceable afterwards ("self-explainable AI", no black box), training data is verified, the model is recalibrated and bias is handled: ČNB benchmark pp. 15 to 16 [G5].
- Automated systems "should never be considered a substitute for staff vigilance": EBA 9.12 [G2].
- An automated risk rating must be overridable in justified cases: Decree §7(4) [CZ2].
- From July 2027, automated decisions on a relationship, a transaction or the due diligence level need "meaningful human intervention": AMLR Art. 76(5) [EU1]. Alert triage alone is not named.
- No source read permits closing an alert with no human check at all. UNVERIFIED.

### Escalation, roles and conflict of interest

| Role                                    | Czech law                                                            | EU and guidance                                                                              |
| --------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Board member responsible for AML        | Act §22a [CZ1]                                                       | AMLD Art. 46(4) [EU2]; AMLR Art. 11(1) compliance manager [EU1]; EBA/GL/2022/05 para 17 [G4] |
| Contact person, files the OPO with FAÚ  | Act §22(1) [CZ1]                                                     | AMLD Art. 33(2) [EU2]; AMLR Art. 11(2), 69(6) compliance officer [EU1]                       |
| Who evaluates and who decides on filing | Named in the bank's internal rules: Act §21(5)(f) [CZ1]; MP 11 [CZ7] | EBA/GL/2022/05 para 52(a) to (c) [G4]                                                        |

- The contact person at a bank must not be an employee responsible for concluding or settling its transactions, or someone in internal audit: Act §22(5) [CZ1]. A board member may not be the contact person unless justified: §22(4).
- Separation of incompatible functions: Decree §16(3)(d) [CZ2]; ČNB p. 11 [G5].
- The compliance function is second line, independent of the business lines, and not subordinate to anyone who manages them: EBA/GL/2022/05 para 31 [G4]. Conflicts are identified and managed: para 27.
- Assessment "ensures objectivity and avoids conflicts of interest": AMLA draft para 77 [EU4].
- The compliance officer documents a prioritisation process and regularly reviews why alerts were not escalated: EBA/GL/2022/05 para 52(a) to (c) and (g) [G4].
- **No four-eyes rule** for alert closure or for filing exists in the Czech Act, the Decree, the AMLD, the AMLR, EBA/GL/2022/05 or the ČNB benchmark. The nearest is a prior approval "by at least one employee one grade above", which is only one of the enhanced due diligence options: Decree §9(2)(c) [CZ2]. The CSV's 4-eyes approver is FINREG's requirement.
- **No rule that an analyst must not review a client they manage** was found. The nearest are §22(5), EBA para 31 and AMLA para 77. UNVERIFIED.

### Deadlines, records and reporting

- The investigation deadline is set by the bank, counted from when it got the triggering information: Decree §17a [CZ2]. ČNB expects days for daily or weekly scenarios and up to a month for long-horizon ones; the clock starts at alert generation, not assignment: ČNB p. 13 [G5].
- Report "without undue delay", or immediately where delay is dangerous: Act §18(1) [CZ1]. FAÚ reads this as shorter than 5 calendar days [CZ4]. The 7-day figure in MP 4 applies only to professional chambers passing reports on. AMLR Art. 69(1): "promptly" [EU1]; FATF R.20 the same [G1].
- The OPO must not name the employee who detected the transaction: Act §18(3) [CZ1].
- Postponement: execute the order no earlier than 24 hours after FAÚ receives the OPO; FAÚ may extend: Act §20 [CZ1][CZ10]. From July 2027, the bank may proceed if the FIU gives no contrary instruction within 3 working days: AMLR Art. 71(1) [EU1].
- Tipping-off: Act §38 [CZ1]; AMLD Art. 39 [EU2]; AMLR Art. 73, which also covers the fact that an assessment is under way [EU1].
- Records must show who performed each step and when, with what result, on what inputs and reasons, and who entered which data: Decree §18(3) [CZ2]. Decisions must be reconstructable: §18(1).
- Retention is 10 years after the relationship ends: Act §16(1)(f) and §16(5) [CZ1]. AMLD and AMLR set 5 years [EU2][EU1].

### Threshold tuning

- Thresholds need a documented rationale, segmentation by client type and client-declared information, and continuous evaluation of effectiveness: ČNB pp. 6 to 7 and footnote 16 [G5].
- Test the system by ex-post review of a sample of processed transactions: EBA 4.75 [G2].
- Even a well-tuned rule-based system shows about 10% effectiveness: ČNB footnote 48 [G5].

### FATF grey list history (rule B)

- South Africa was under increased monitoring from 24 February 2023 to 24 October 2025 [G10].
- Türkiye was under increased monitoring from October 2021 to the June 2024 plenary [G8][G9].
- Neither is on the 19 June 2026 list [G7]. The dataset's country mix fits a list from 2023 to 2024.

## Still UNVERIFIED

- A four-eyes requirement for alert closure or filing, in any source.
- A bar on an analyst assessing a client they manage.
- Any source allowing closure with no human check.
- Rule C as a named typology.
- The Decree's post-2021 amendment number; AMLR Art. 77(2) operative text; TFR Art. 4(3) and 6; FATF R.16 detail; EBA Title I paras 2.20 and 2.21 (seen only in the EBA response table).
